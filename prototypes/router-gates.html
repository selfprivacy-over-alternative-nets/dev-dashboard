Proposal: the router as a gatehouse, the phonebook, and when Direct IPv6 works

Every animation loops: first your friend's request (the white light) reaches your server; then a request hits a shut gate — it is stopped at the fire wall (it turns red and bounces).

1. The story in one picture — a phone analogy

In the diagramPlain words (the analogy)
Domain name / DNS📖 The phonebook: your friend looks up "yourname" and finds your number.
Registrar (bought domain)📖🐢 A slow phonebook: you change your number by hand on their website (most registrars, e.g. theory7).
DuckDNS📖⚡ A fast phonebook: your server updates its own entry automatically, within minutes — free.
IP address☎️ The phone number. IPv4: the whole house shares one number. IPv6: every device has its own.
Port-forward (NAT, IPv4)🚧 The receptionist (barrier gate): "calls for extension 443 go to the server".
Router firewall🔥 The doorman (a wall with flames — a fire wall): refuses every unknown caller unless you put the server on the list.
Tunnel (Tailscale, Cloudflare, Tor)📞 The server calls out to a helper and keeps the line open; friends call the helper, who passes it on. The tunnel runs over both gates — nothing to open.
  • Port-forward (IPv4): open both gates — the doorman lets port 443 in, the receptionist sends it to the server.
  • Direct IPv6 is not a tunnel: the server has its own number, so there is no receptionist — but the doorman still blocks callers until you open the fire wall for it. Only works for a server at home, on a router whose settings you can change — not for a laptop moving from the supermarket to a café (new number every place, and the café's doorman never lets your callers in).
  • Tunnels: both gates stay shut; the tunnel goes over them, because your server started the call.

2. The phonebook (replaces "Buy a domain" / "DuckDNS" as boxes)

Your friend never types your ip: they look up https://yourdomain.org in a phonebook and get the ip to go to. When your ip changes, the phonebook must be updated: by you, by hand on the website of the 🐌 slow phonebook (most registrars, where you bought the domain), or automatically by your server in the ⚡ fast phonebook (DuckDNS). The book's colour is its cost, like everywhere in the diagram: red = costs money, orange = a service you depend on. Direct IPv6 and port-forward need a phonebook that keeps up; the tunnels don't (the helper's address never changes).

The DNS phonebook as an icon (demo): one lookup

The white light is the https address your friend asks for. It goes into the book, the book opens and flips to its page, finds the ip that matches, packs that ip into a gray light and sends it on to your server.
